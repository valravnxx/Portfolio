WARWORKS

AndroidIdle + factoryF2PGodot 4Closed beta

A factory licensed by the Ministry of Defence. They set the contracts; you lay the belts. A factory game with real production chains, in a bright low-poly toy style, played vertically with one thumb.

My role: solo game designer, director and tester · implementation AI-assisted · trailer and screenshots rendered from the current build

71recipes from 8 raw materials to 10 end products
12.6 hmeasured minimum play time, from simulation
16automated checks before every build
900+texts verified in English inside the running game

Design

  • 5 production branches and 11 licences, from mortars to fighter jets and missiles; vehicles travel by rail, parts by belt.
  • Multi-factory industrial map with player-built roads and trucks between factories.
  • The Ministry: 8 contracts, rush orders, daily orders, audits and operations that keep old production lines useful.
  • Prestige across 6 nations, each with its own rule; the last one gives half your land to the army.
  • The game tells you what's wrong: jammed belts light up red, a stopped machine names the part it's missing, and the production sheet points at the real bottleneck.
  • Fair F2P: no artificial waits, no paywalls, no forced ads; everything can be completed without paying.

QA

  • Closed beta with testers: player reports are reproduced, fixed and covered by a new test.
  • Every new test is checked by breaking the code on purpose, to prove it actually catches the bug.
  • 11 test batteries (simulation, grid, languages, values, throughput, play time, fuzzing…) plus 5 tests that tap the running game with synthetic touches.
  • Fuzzer: 30,000 random player actions per run, checking the game never breaks its own rules.
  • An accessibility round driven by tester feedback, rebuilding how doors and connections are explained.

Balancing story: simulate the whole game, don't guess

I built a simulation that plays the entire game with the real data tables: the player buys their bottleneck and waits when they can't afford it. The first run showed that contracts paid about 5× what the factory earned, that contract 2 paid worse than contract 1, and that contracts didn't progress while the game was closed. After rebalancing, contracts pay about 0.9× the factory and the measured minimum play time is 12.6 hours. The simulation reruns every time a number changes.

THE INTEGRATOR

AndroidTurn-based PvE extractionFirst person, gridGodot 4In development

You are Operator 07. Your salvage drones go down into a derelict hull full of machines still doing their jobs. The drones carry no weapons: you build them from the tech you bring back. The question is never "can I hit it?" but "do I have the charge to get back?"

My role: solo game designer, director and tester · implementation AI-assisted · screenshots and video rendered from the current build (UI in Spanish)

▶ Play the demo in your browserSpanish UI · desktop: arrow keys to move, 1 · 2 · 3 for the three buttons, space to wait · phone: touch controls
7procedurally generated zones
49drone parts in 5 slots, 3 tiers each
24action verbs that rotate every turn
73automated tests run before every build, all green

Design

  • The turn is the currency: walking, hitting and searching all cost the same charge, so fighting is expensive and running away is legitimate.
  • Verbs rotate instead of being chosen. Enemies' cycles are visible too, so combat is about lining up your cycle with the gap in theirs.
  • Tier-2 and tier-3 parts need a component that only one specific enemy drops: no fighting, no upgrades.
  • Reward preparation, don't punish failure: the standard drone is always replaced for free; what you lose is what you bolted onto it.
  • The map tells the truth: exits are marked from turn 0, and anything that blocks the way is drawn as blocking.
  • Two bosses built as boards, not health bars: they don't move, but change the floor every turn and show the tiles they'll hit.

QA

  • Every design claim becomes an assertion, checked by bots that play real runs.
  • A test that passes first time gets broken on purpose to prove it can fail.
  • Flaky tests are bugs: one read an enemy's rotation mid-animation and failed 6 times out of 19.
  • Touch targets measured against Android's 48 dp guideline: the map tabs went from 15 dp to 43 dp tall.
  • Audio checked for phone speakers: an ambience with 61% of its energy below 200 Hz was cut to 34%.

Balancing story: the enemy that hit every other turn

The Shell had a two-step cycle, so it attacked every other turn: 2.00 damage per turn, double the Mule's, and it spawns in sleeping groups. Two of them next to you killed a full-health drone in three turns. I kept its 4-damage hit, because that's its identity, but added a second crawl before it: 1.33 damage per turn and two turns of warning, enough to see it coming and break contact.

DRAGON FIGHTING LEAGUE

AndroidSports sim · fake-money bettingFree + PremiumGodot 4Release in preparation

A league of procedurally generated dragons that fight twice a day in real time, and a betting app built around it, with play money only. Every phone computes the same league, so friends watch the same fight live. The Premium edition adds a ranch where you breed, train and enter your own dragons.

My role: solo game designer, director and tester · implementation AI-assisted · screenshots and the fight clip rendered from the current build (v0.13.4)

A whole league fight, exported by the app's built-in GIF recorder
1league for everyone: same fights, same results on every phone
50dragons in 3 age divisions that age, retire and get replaced
5 yearsof league simulated to test that it stays healthy
3languages: English, Spanish and Russian

Design

  • Real-time schedule: a midday and a prime-time card every day, a title fight on Saturday nights, bets open until the bell.
  • Odds come only from fight history (rating plus an 8% house edge). They know nothing about bodies or elements, and that's where a player who reads the tale of the tape finds an edge.
  • A newsroom that writes its own headlines from what happened: upsets, title changes, streaks, debuts, retirements.
  • Premium ranch: design an egg, train each dragon at the hours its nature prefers, pick its opponents in a private league, and add friends' dragons by code.
  • Everything you can buy is cosmetic (arenas, themes, frames, titles): nothing helps you win bets.

QA

  • Determinism as a requirement: live, replay and result are the same fight from the same seed, and shared fight codes reproduce it on a friend's phone.
  • A long-run simulation found the league emptying out (after one year: 7 seniors, no veterans, over half of card slots empty). After new rules, five simulated years: full divisions, 10 empty slots out of 15,122.
  • The newsroom's headlines are checked by tests against what actually happened.
  • Flight measured, not guessed: across the same 100 random fights, a flyer against a non-flyer won 41% with the old movement engine and 59% with the new one.
  • 9 test suites green before this build, from the betting flow to names and editions.

Balancing story: a league that slowly died

Retired dragons were only replaced by youngsters, who take months to reach senior. A one-year simulation showed 43 youths, 7 seniors and no veterans, with most fight slots empty. I changed the rules: each division refills to its size with signings of its age, newcomers enter at the average rating, and old cards are forgotten after 40 days. Five simulated years later the divisions stay full and nobody waits more than 14 days for a fight.

Sample bug reports

Real defects from my own projects, written the way I'd file them in a studio.

WW-077 · WARWORKS betaCritical

Removing an entry door deletes every belt in the factory

Found by
A beta player, with two screenshots.
Steps
  1. Build a factory with belts, a splitter and machines.
  2. Remove one of its entry doors.
Expected
Only the door is removed.
Actual
Every belt, splitter and tunnel disappears; machines stay floating on an empty floor. Reproduced first time: 6 transport cells → 0.
Cause
Removing a door rebuilt the whole grid to renumber doors, and belts only exist in the grid.
Fix
Renumber only the door cells. Two new tests, both confirmed to fail with the old code.
TI-022 · The IntegratorCritical

Soft-lock: a dormant enemy can block the only path to the exit

Found by
Metrics bot, 2 of 10 seeds.
Steps
  1. Generate a map with an affected seed.
  2. Follow the only route towards the exit.
Expected
Every generated map can be completed.
Actual
A dormant enemy blocks the corridor and cannot be attacked, so the run cannot be finished.
Fix
The map validator now treats dormant enemies as walls and removes any that block the route.
TI-MAP · The IntegratorHigh

The map draws blocking furniture as walkable floor

Found by
Map audit on a test seed.
Steps
  1. Explore a floor with cabinets or benches in the corridors.
  2. Open the map on the drone's PDA.
Expected
Anything that blocks movement is drawn hatched, as blocking.
Actual
15 pieces of furniture on one floor drawn as open floor: 32% of the explored area. In a 1-wide corridor that's a dead end the map calls a path.
Cause
Furniture lives in its own table, not in the map grid the PDA was reading.
Fix
The map now reads blocking from the same data the movement code uses.
WW-088 · WARWORKS betaCritical

Store purchases are granted for free outside the editor

Found by
A beta tester who ended up with 219,450 premium bonds without paying.
Steps
  1. Install a build without the store's billing plugin.
  2. Buy any pack of bonds.
Expected
The purchase fails honestly.
Actual
The pack is granted instantly; rewarded ads had the same hole.
Cause
A shortcut for testing the store in the editor was active in every build.
Fix
The shortcut only works in debug builds; a release-check test now guards it.

How I work

I design, I direct, I test. The code is AI-assisted, and I'm upfront about it.

Design first

I decide the systems, the numbers and the rules, and I write down what each one is supposed to achieve so it can be checked.

Nothing ships untested

Every build passes automated tests before I play it on a real device. When a bug slips through, it gets a test so it can't come back.

Data over hunches

Bots play whole runs and report the numbers. If a design claim doesn't show up in the data, the design changes.
